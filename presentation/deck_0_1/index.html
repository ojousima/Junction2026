The problem

Millions of heat pumps and ACs run outdoors,
unattended, until they fail

1.2 M+

heat pumps in use in Finland, producing about one fifth of building heat

~10 %

of world electricity goes to space cooling, and about 30 % of peak demand

200 M

air conditioners shipped worldwide in 2024 alone, most never monitored

€140 B/yr

equipment market in 2024: ≈ €90 B+ ACs, ≈ €45 B heat pumps

  • Slow drift: dirt, leaves and pollen clog the outdoor coil. Efficiency falls a little every month. Nobody notices.
  • Failed defrost: ice stays on the coil in winter and the unit falls back to direct electric heating.
  • Mechanical faults: a twig in the fan, a worn bearing or an unbalanced blade, heard only when it is already loud.
  • No feedback: the owner sees only a higher bill, or a cold house on the coldest night of the year.
Sources: SULPU via Yrittäjät (2022); IEA Space Cooling briefing; IEA, The Future of Cooling; IEA heat pump market ≈ USD 50 B (2024); AC market USD 99–200 B depending on analyst (Grand View Research low end used), converted at ≈ 0.9 €/USD.

The impact

Hidden losses cost money, and cost the climate more

Financial

  • Faults can raise heating and cooling energy use by up to ~30 % (NIST).
  • A missed fault turns a cheap cleaning into a compressor or fan motor repair.
  • Breakdowns hit in mid-winter, when service is slowest and backup heat is most expensive.
Example house: 10 000 kWh/yr heat-pump electricity
× 15 % efficiency loss × 0.15 €/kWh
≈ 225 € / year, per house, unnoticed

Environmental

kWh

Wasted electricity is wasted CO₂. Peaks are covered by fossil reserve plants, and dirty coils and ice push demand up exactly at the cold, high-carbon peak hours.

COP 1

Failed defrost or breakdown means resistive backup. The same heat then needs about 3× more electricity than a healthy heat pump.

2 t

CO₂e per kg of leaked R410A (GWP 2088). Units that run hot under fault stress their seals and joints. Early warning keeps refrigerant inside.

Life

Longer equipment life. Each premature replacement adds manufacturing emissions and e-waste.

Sources: Domanski et al., NIST TN 1848 (2014); IPCC AR4 GWP for R410A; EU F-gas Regulation (EU) 2024/573. The 225 € example is our own estimate.

The solution

Plug-and-play sensor box under the outdoor unit

  • Sits under the outdoor unit. Camera and microphone look and listen at the fan and coil.
  • Two wired probes, one on each side of the coil: air in and air out. The temperature gradient across the coil is the health signal.
  • All magnet attachments. No drilling, no tools, no refrigerant work, no warranty risk. Install in 5 minutes.
  • Brand-agnostic retrofit for any heat pump or AC, old or new.
  • Edge AI on Arduino UNO Q. All analysis runs on the device. No cloud backend: raw audio and images never leave the house.
4
The diagnostics

Three senses, one health score

Thermal

Dirty coil, weak defrost

  • Coil fouling: temperature gradient across the coil drifts away from the healthy value at the same operating point.
  • Defrost not effective: gradient does not recover after a defrost cycle.
  • Slow trend tracked over weeks, with an uncertainty band.
Healthy model per operating point, Kalman filter fouling estimate, CUSUM alarm
Audio

Fan and motor faults

  • Object touching the fan: twig, leaf or ice scraping the blades, a ticking at blade-pass rate.
  • Bearing wear: rising high-frequency noise and new harmonics.
  • Imbalance: strong peak at the rotation frequency, rattle and vibration.
16 kHz audio, FFT band energies, on-device anomaly model
Visual

Ice and snow

  • Ice on the coil that defrost does not clear.
  • Snow build-up blocking the intake or fan grille.
  • Ice on the fan, cross-checked with the audio channel.
USB camera, periodic frame, icing and frost score
